# Initial bench procedure — supervised one-channel prototype

**No speakers. Use the TTI bench supply first. No output short-circuit protection is fitted; do not short the output.** This is a development procedure, not a release certificate. The current bias network fails a combined low-threshold/hot model corner (about 49 mA cold to 263 mA at 75 °C). Do not treat pot adjustment or heatsink attachment as a fix. Characterize the actual pair and resolve the thermal-compensation issue before ordering/assembling the final amplifier PCB. Resolve the package-fit checks and understand the bias uncertainty in `DESIGN.md` first. A correctly mounted heatsink is required even for idle testing. Add a contact temperature probe before thermal qualification; the listed scope, DMM, generator and supply alone cannot measure heatsink temperature reliably.

## Before power

1. Inspect every fitted value and polarity against the schematic and BOM. Check continuity of all rails and GND, then check for low resistance between each rail/GND and across the rails. Reservoir capacitors can make resistance readings move. Check no solder bridges, especially MOSFET G/D/S and the 2012 metric input capacitors. Verify BC546/556 C-B-E, BD139/140 E-C-B and IRF G-D-S using the actual manufacturer's drawing for the parts on the bench.
2. Check the actual source resistors are 0R33, not 0R033/3R3. Lead resistance limits a two-wire ohms test; subtract shorted-lead resistance. Confirm the selected power-resistor and pot bodies fit without forced leads. Electrically verify the air coil is continuous and cannot touch the heatsink; measure inductance when an LCR meter becomes available.
3. Insulate both output drain tabs from the common heatsink, including screws/shoulders. Insulate the Q108/Q208 collector tabs as well. Any driver tab sharing that sink also needs insulation. Before connecting external earthed cables, check each live tab to sink reads open/high resistance (target >20 MΩ if the DMM supports it), and inspect for mica burrs/tears. Repeat after tightening. A DMM isolation check does not replace visual inspection.
4. Set RV101 to **maximum measured resistance** between pin 1 and pins 2+3, approximately 2 kΩ. Confirm pin 2 is physically tied to pin 3. Do not assume clockwise or anticlockwise direction. Fit a shunt to J101 to short **BP to BN**. The corresponding parts on RIGHT are RV201/J201. Operate/remount shunts only with power off and rails discharged.
5. Short the active channel's input to GND at J3 (J4 for RIGHT). Leave its output load disconnected initially. Power one channel only; leave the other power connector unconnected and its input undriven. Use TP101–103 for source resistor measurements, TP104/105 for rails; repeat +100 on RIGHT. Connect the meter before enabling power.

## Bench supply and scope connections

Verify the EL302RD manual's isolated-output series capability and use independent current limits. For two isolated outputs in series: channel 1 positive goes to amplifier +V; channel 1 negative joins channel 2 positive at amplifier **GND**; channel 2 negative goes to amplifier −V. Check all three voltages with the DMM before connecting the amplifier. Both rails must rise together. A supply entering current limit on one rail can create a large output offset; switch both off promptly rather than leaving one collapsed.

The DOS1102 BNC shells/probe ground clips and a conventional generator ground are earthed. Connect all such grounds only to amplifier **GND**. Never attach a scope ground clip to −V, SN, SP, a gate, or across a source resistor. Use the floating DMM across source resistors, or properly referenced differential scope subtraction with both ground clips at GND. Check the generator's 50 Ω/Hi-Z amplitude convention. Use ×10 probes with very short ground connections; start at OUT and SPK. Probing a gate adds capacitance and can change stability.

## First DC checks

1. Set approximately **±8 V, 50 mA current limit per rail**, with the bias shunt fitted. This is a brief commissioning condition below the intended operating range. Enable both rails while watching the current and output. The linked development model draws about 8–11 mA/rail, with negligible MOSFET current; use this as an order-of-magnitude check, not an exact pass criterion.
2. Check actual rail voltages, GND, output DC and voltage across both source resistors. Switch off immediately if either rail remains in current limit, DC output exceeds ±100 mV, source-resistor voltage is unexpectedly appreciable with the shunt fitted, or any device warms quickly. Diagnose before raising the limit. A fault can discharge local capacitors even with current limiting.
3. With the shunt still fitted, raise to **±12 V**, keeping 50 mA limits. Repeat. Inspect output with the scope at full bandwidth and a short ground spring for ultrasonic oscillation, first without a load and then with the 8 Ω dummy load. Avoid confusing long-probe pickup with a real oscillation; confirm at OUT and SPK. The shunt condition is deliberately underbiased and will not reproduce normal audio operation.
4. Turn both bench-supply outputs off and wait until DMM readings are below 1 V on both rails. Remove the bias shunt. Keep RV101 at maximum resistance. Restart at **±12 V, 75–100 mA limits** while monitoring one source resistor and rail currents. If minimum trim already gives excessive bias, shut down. Do not turn the pot blindly to find an apparent minimum: characterize VGS and revise the range deliberately. A sufficiently low-threshold pair can be overbiased even at this nominal minimum setting.

## Bias adjustment and thermal tracking

Near conduction, use movements of about 1/64 turn initially and watch the meter continuously; the nominal adjustment is near the low-resistance end of the 25-turn track. Advance the trimmer only a small amount in the direction that **reduces resistance and raises bias**, established with the ohmmeter before power. First aim for 3.3 mV across each 0R33 (10 mA), then 8.25 mV (25 mA), checking for HF oscillation at each step. Recheck the other source resistor. At ±15 V the final provisional target is **16.5 mV (50 mA)** after warming; do not copy the SPICE trimmer value. A small N/P difference near 1 mA can occur from other circuit currents.

Raise rails gradually to ±15 V only after stable operation at ±12 V. Rail current normally rises by the signal-stage current in addition to output bias: the nominal model at ±15 V/50 mA bias draws about 62 mA/rail. A 100–125 mA limit is suitable for idle adjustments after the initial checks. Keep the DMM connected to a source resistor while observing the scope. **33 mV (100 mA) is a stop threshold**, not a target. Stop if bias rises continuously, offset exceeds ±100 mV, oscillation appears, a rail current limit activates, or the sink exceeds 60 °C. Check both devices/sensors with a temperature probe; never use touch as the temperature measurement.

Allow 30–60 minutes of supervised warm-up. Record source drops, rail currents, rail voltages, output offset and sink temperature at 0/1/5/15/30/60 minutes. Aim for stable warm 40–60 mA without a continuing upward trend. Repeat from cold after cooling. The sensor must be secured at its final insulated heatsink location; moving it invalidates the result. Do not compensate unstable bias by repeatedly turning it down during warm-up and then calling it stable.

Before qualifying bias, separately measure the actual N/P devices' VGS at about 50 mA and at roughly 1 A, at cold and elevated controlled temperatures, using a current-limited fixture. The multiplier's thermal slope/range may require revision. This characterization and hot-SOA evidence remain outstanding; unattended operation is not authorized by a successful quick idle test.

## Signal checks into 8 Ω

1. Connect the high-power 8 Ω dummy load and a 1 kHz sine. Start with about 20 mV peak input (roughly 0.21 V peak output). Check phase, gain, DC level and a smooth zero crossing. A scope cannot prove low THD; use an audio analyser or suitable calibrated sound-card/attenuator arrangement later for actual distortion measurement.
2. Repeat at 20 Hz and 20 kHz at low level. Check there is no HF burst near zero crossings or clipping. Test 1 kHz and 10 kHz square waves at 50–100 mV input peak, then up to 0.2 V peak if clean. Look at OUT and SPK for sustained ringing/oscillation, not only a rounded screenshot. Use the scope's bandwidth limit only after checking full bandwidth.
3. Increase sine amplitude gradually at ±12 V, then ±15 V. Increase each rail current limit from about 0.2 A to 0.5 A, then approximately 1.0–1.5 A only after the low-power checks pass and within the supply rating. If current limiting occurs, that waveform is a supply-limited result, not the amplifier's clipping power. Avoid operation with one rail collapsed. Note positive and negative clip peaks separately. For an unclipped sine, P=Vrms²/8 or Vpk²/16; check generator and scope scaling.
4. Expect the model to run out of gate headroom around 5.6 V peak/2 W at ±12 V and 8.4 V peak/4.5 W at ±15 V. Actual thresholds and rail sag may reduce this. Do not force 10 W from ±15 V. Briefly inspect clipping/recovery, then return to small signal and confirm offset/bias recover. Do not dwell in overload or make an output-short test.
5. After resistive-load stability is established, try realistic speaker cable or 1–10 nF parallel capacitance with low-amplitude square waves. Use short insulated load connections. Extreme 100 nF/reactive simulation tests are analysis stress cases, not the first bench load. A speaker's nominal 8 Ω rating does not guarantee its minimum impedance or phase angle; review the intended speaker before testing it.
6. Exercise moderate continuous sine power and then return to idle while watching bias. Maximum transistor heating occurs before maximum output power, approximately Vpk=0.64×rail for an ideal class-B stage; inspect several amplitudes. Record sink temperature, current and recovery over the full warm-up interval. Stop at the criteria above. Repeat each channel separately, then stereo together to evaluate shared-sink and PSU interaction.

## Final PSU and speaker connection

Do not begin with the transformer supply. When a PSU is selected, measure unloaded and loaded rails first, with mains variation assumptions and capacitor tolerances accounted for. If either rail can exceed ±21 V, stop and recalculate. Repeat bias, clipping, ripple, startup/shutdown and thermal tests using the actual PSU. Reservoir capacitance makes faults more energetic than the initial bench setup.

Observe output during power-on/off with the scope and dummy load. Approximate simulations show several tenths of a volt and over 1 V for asymmetric rail ramps; the real relay/protection board must remain disconnected during these intervals. Verify that board's DC detection, delay, disconnect time and grounding before attaching speakers. It does not protect these MOSFETs against an output short. This revision does not support 4 Ω operation. Connect speakers only after the documented electrical/thermal issues have been resolved and the external protection has been tested.
