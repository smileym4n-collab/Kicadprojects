# Electrical design and calculations — Rev 0.1 experimental

**Disposition: connected, simulated development schematic; NOT released for PCB fabrication or unattended operation.** Manufacturer MOSFET/driver models could not be obtained. A combined low-threshold/hot model corner fails the bias requirement; actual low-current VGS/temperature tracking and hot linear-mode SOA remain unverified. The supplied approximations establish operating feasibility and expose sensitivities; they do not establish a hi-fi performance guarantee. No output short-circuit protection is fitted, as requested. The intended prototype load is 8 Ω.

## Architecture and alternatives

Each channel uses a PNP differential pair with 100 Ω emitter degeneration, NPN current-mirror load, approximately 2 mA tail source, buffered NPN VAS, approximately 6 mA PNP active load, BD139/BD140 emitter-follower gate drivers and one IRF540N/IRF9540N complementary source-follower pair. A single global voltage-feedback loop senses OUT before the output isolation coil. The signal and feedback path are DC coupled; there is no servo, bootstrap supply or regulator. The reference numbering is +100 for LEFT and +200 for RIGHT; the description below uses LEFT.

A resistor-loaded input/VAS would save transistors but gives poorer open-loop gain and rail rejection while still needing nearly the same gate headroom. A single unbuffered VAS loads the input pair with driver/base current and complicates compensation. The small Q115 buffer is retained for that reason. A complementary VAS or bootstrapped driver supply could improve symmetry/headroom, with more parts and clipping/startup cases. A common-source/CFP output would change loop behaviour and require a different design. An IC input stage is simpler but does not meet the fully discrete preference. The selected conventional three-stage circuit is a reasonable low-voltage prototype; it does not extract 10 W from ±15 V.

D101/D102 and R108 form an unregulated two-junction reference. Q105 with 330 Ω sets the tail to roughly VBE/330 ≈ 1.8–2 mA; each input transistor operates near 1 mA. Q107 uses 100 Ω for roughly 6 mA. These currents vary with rail voltage, temperature and part spread; the SPICE operating-point files contain actual nominal-model values. Q106's 100 Ω emitter resistor sets its current near this load current; Q115 isolates its base current from the mirror node. D107 and 100 V Schottky BAT46 D108 clamp the VAS drive-to-collector relationship before deep saturation. D105/D106 limit differential input overload. These are recovery provisions, not input overvoltage immunity. The stocked 1N5819 is only 40 V; BAT46 is purchased to give reverse-voltage margin against the possible 42 V bus. The manufacturer rates BAT46 at 100 V, 150 mA and 150 mW with specified lead cooling, with a maximum 0.45 V forward drop at 10 mA. Normal clamp current is of order a few milliamps. Its small DO-35 body and cathode band match the assigned footprint.

R103=22 kΩ and R104=2.2 kΩ give gain 11 (20.83 dB) at B1. The 1 kΩ series input resistor and 22 kΩ shunt give external gain approximately 11×22/23 = **10.52 (20.44 dB)** and input impedance about **23 kΩ**, for a low-impedance source. The input base sees about 957 Ω Thevenin resistance for a zero-impedance source; the feedback base sees 2 kΩ. These are not an exact bias-current balance, so base-current and mirror errors contribute to offset. Select Q101/Q102 for close VBE (preferably within 1 mV) and similar gain at 1 mA, thermally bond their flat faces and use the same gain rank when possible. A 1 mV input-pair mismatch produces approximately 11 mV additional output offset. Nominal-model offset is 6.5 mV, not a production tolerance. DC from the source is amplified too; a 2 Vrms line source will need upstream attenuation. Model input at first significant clipping at ±15 V is about **0.56 Vrms**.

C101=220 pF with the input network has a rough input pole around 0.75 MHz for a zero-impedance source. C102=330 pF C0G is the dominant-pole compensation, from C2N to BN. The approximate loop first crosses unity near 300 kHz. Increasing C102 from the preliminary 220 pF to 330 pF improves the severe reactive/parasitic case first-crossing margin from roughly 46° to 66°, at the cost of some bandwidth/slew rate. The final part is a purchased through-hole KEMET C315C331J5G5TA (50 V, 5%, C0G), 2.54 mm nominal pitch, maximum body 3.81×2.54 mm, with a project-local footprint based on the manufacturer C1049 drawing. This is a justified compensation purchase rather than an awkward combination of stocked values. The two 100 Ω gate stoppers damp local MOSFET parasitics; do not replace them with links. Zobel = 10 Ω/2 W in series with 100 nF film from OUT to GND. The **0.7 µH coil with parallel 10 Ω/2 W damping resistor** is outside feedback and retained because it reduces the load presented to the loop by capacitive cables. At 20 kHz XL≈0.088 Ω, small relative to 8 Ω. Its exact inductance is a measured build parameter. R115/R116 keep the BJT drivers conducting at about 1.5–2 mA at idle.

## MOSFET selection and limits

The primary pair remains IRF540N/IRF9540N. The owned IRFZ44N are not used. Low RDS(on) and headline switching-current ratings are not used as audio power ratings.

| Parameter | IRF540N | IRF9540N |
|---|---:|---:|
| VDS absolute maximum | +100 V | −100 V |
| VGS absolute maximum | ±20 V | ±20 V |
| Junction maximum | 175 °C | 175 °C |
| RθJC maximum | 1.15 K/W | 1.1 K/W |
| Headline PD at case 25 °C, not free air | 130 W | 140 W |
| Threshold at 250 µA | 2–4 V | magnitude 2–4 V |
| Typical Ciss / Coss / Crss at datasheet test bias | 1960 / 250 / 40 pF | 1300 / 400 / 240 pF |
| Maximum Qg / Qgd at stated high-current switching test | 71 / 21 nC | 97 / 51 nC |
| TO-220 pins / tab | 1 G, 2 D, 3 S / D | 1 G, 2 D, 3 S / D |

These are from the manufacturer PDFs saved alongside this note. Capacitances and gate charge depend strongly on operating voltage; they are not constants over an audio waveform. The P device's much greater Crss/Qgd and lower transconductance make it appreciably different from the N device. Equal gate resistors do not make them electrically symmetric. The newer IRF540N PDF and older IRF9540N PDF are not a matched characterization set.

Threshold is specified at microamps, not the gate voltage for useful load current. Figure 3 transfer plots are typical, pulsed and primarily at much higher currents than the intended 50 mA idle point. They do not guarantee low-current matching or temperature coefficients. The development models use Id≈KP/2×(VGS−VTO)², with VTO 3.4/3.77 V and KP 20/7.07 A/V² for N/P. That yields approximate VGS at 50 mA of 3.47/3.89 V and at 1.2 A of 3.75/4.35 V before series drops and channel-length modulation. These are **model estimates**, not datasheet guarantees or a reason to preset the bias voltage. Measure the purchased devices at 50 mA and about 1 A before treating them as characterized.

With source resistance, BJT VBE and active-load/VAS compliance, approximately **6–7 V of each rail is unavailable for speaker swing** near clipping. The final per-rail, load-dependent result is obtained from simulation, not simply (rail−4 V)²/(2R). The 100 V rating allows a 42 V nominal bus at the assumed ceiling, but voltage rating alone says nothing about linear SOA. The 10 V gate-source Zeners clamp forward gate drive and conduct in the reverse polarity; they are local VGS safeguards, not output fault protection.

## Bias and temperature — required commissioning work

D109 (4.3 V) in series with the Q108 BD139 multiplier supplies the large bias spread required by two vertical MOSFET gates plus two BJT driver junctions. A simple multiplier set to the whole approximately 8.6 V spread would have excessive VBE multiplication and temperature dependence. The added low-voltage Zener reduces this ratio; 4.3 V is selected instead of the owned 5.1 V part so a substantial thermally sensed VBE multiplication remains. A Zener alone would provide inadequate thermal tracking. A fixed pair of diodes is inadequate for these devices. A separate servo that measures source current could control idle current more tightly but adds sensing/loop/startup complexity. It is not included.

Let RE = 47k || (130 + RV), with RV from 0 to 2 kΩ. To first order:

`Vspread ≈ VZ + VBE × (1 + 1k/RE) + IB × 1k`.

At RE≈167 Ω, multiplier≈7.0. Taking VBE≈0.60 V gives 4.3+4.2≈8.5 V plus the base-current term; the actual nominal-model spread is about 8.59 V. The nominal model adjusts to about **50 mA** at ±15 V. This is a low initial compromise between crossover behaviour and dissipation: each 0R33 drops **16.5 mV**, each output device dissipates about 0.75 W at ±15 V before signal. It is not claimed to be the distortion optimum for arbitrary production devices. Start lower and measure distortion/stability before raising it. Nominal power-stage gm is of order √(2KP×Iq), approximately 1.4 S N and 0.84 S P at 50 mA; this confirms the asymmetry and why a lateral-MOSFET bias rule should not be copied.

The intended sensor coefficient is of order 7.0×(−2 mV/K)≈−14 mV/K, plus the real 4.3 V Zener's current-dependent temperature coefficient and reference-current effects. Two MOSFET VGS magnitudes may together require around −8 mV/K and the two driver VBE another −4 mV/K at one operating point, but neither is fixed. Transconductance falls with mobility while threshold generally falls with temperature below the zero-temperature-coefficient current. Consequently gate-drive voltage must usually fall as these switching MOSFETs warm at low idle current. Their much higher-current transfer-curve crossover does not make 50 mA self-stabilizing.

The Zener approximation does not include a qualified temperature coefficient; the BD and MOSFET approximations do not prove that −14 mV/K matches the actual assembled pair. Imposed 2/4/6 mV/K threshold-change sweeps per MOSFET produce roughly **0.08/32/108 mA** at 75 °C with the same nominal adjustment. A cold sensor with 75 °C MOSFETs produces roughly **227 mA**. The combined low-threshold case is worse: with 2 V nominal thresholds and maximum RV, the model rises from about 49 mA at 27 °C to **263 mA at 75 °C**, even with sensor and driver temperatures tracking. At that much lower required DC spread, the multiplier ratio falls and provides too little thermal compensation. **This corner fails; individual adjustment and good heatsink coupling alone do not solve it.** The circuit is therefore not a generally qualified bias solution over the full threshold spread. Device selection/thermal characterization and possibly revised compensation are required before release. Measure actual VGS-versus-temperature and revise the spreader ratio/range if necessary before release. Do not compensate arbitrary parts merely by raising idle current.

Mount Q108 electrically insulated, thermally clamped between/near the two output devices on their heatsink. Track the pair separately for each channel. Thermal coupling reduces steady-state error, but does not remove die-to-sink thermal lag during a sudden signal burst. Driver temperature matters too. Check cold start, warm idle, prolonged near-maximum-dissipation signal, return to idle and cooling. Adjust each channel independently to a provisional warm 40–60 mA only after it is stable; stop if it exceeds 100 mA or keeps rising. These are test limits, not proven component ratings.

RV101 pins 2 and 3 are wired together. **Maximum resistance between pin 1 and pins 2+3 is minimum bias.** A lost wiper contact leaves the full track; an open track removes the adjustment branch, leaving 47 kΩ and reducing spread further. This is fail-low for these specified open-circuit failures. It does not protect against a shorted pot, shorted 130 Ω resistor, failed sensor or failed semiconductor. Nominal minimum spread is about 5.38 V; an open branch about 5.09 V; maximum spread about 9.48 V. Model RV=0 gives approximately 0.63 A and is unsafe to select blindly. Full trimmer range is not a guaranteed safe bias range.

The 1 kΩ/47 kΩ/130 Ω resistor selection gives a wide low-bias adjustment range. A minimum-threshold 2 V/2 V model pair draws about 49 mA at maximum RV, while the nominal pair is essentially off. Because VTH, Zener and temperature spread are broad, maximum RV **still cannot guarantee zero current for every possible pair**. J101 shorts BP to BN for a defined output-off first check. Fit it before first power-up. Remove it only with rails discharged and bench current limiting active on restart. If minimum bias is already excessive, stop and characterize the devices; revise the fixed range resistor deliberately. Do not use a simulated trim resistance as an assembly setting. The nominal setting occupies only about half a turn from one end of a 25-turn 2 kΩ track: use an insulated fine tool, tiny movements (about 1/64 turn initially near conduction) and continuous meter observation. The large total range is for device spread; it is not a safe blind sweep. Fixed-trim sweeps show 29–76 mA for ±1% bias resistors, 46–73 mA for deliberately broad driver/multiplier beta assumptions and up to about 0.72 A when both MOSFET thresholds are shifted down 0.5 V. They justify adjustment and supervised bring-up, not population yield claims.

Measure N idle current between TP101 (SN, meter +) and TP102 (OUT, meter −). Measure P idle current between TP102 (+) and TP103 (SP, −). Repeat with TP201–203 on RIGHT. Use `I = V/0.33`, with no input and load disconnected. There is about 1 mA normal N/P measurement difference from other paths/offset, not necessarily defective devices. 3.3/8.25/16.5/33 mV correspond to 10/25/50/100 mA. The finite source-resistor tolerance and meter/lead errors limit accuracy; match the four resistors reasonably rather than pretending millivolts are exact.

## Supply envelope — conditional assumptions, not transformer specifications

Approved assumptions: 230 V primary; 12-0-12 VAC specified at full rated load; 100 VA; up to 10% no-load regulation; 50 Hz; bridge across the entire 24 VAC winding with centre tap as GND; 4,700 µF per rail. Each bus charging path has two bridge diode drops **across both rails**, equivalent to about one drop per rail for a balanced centre reference. A PSU arranged differently must be recalculated. No rectifier or mains wiring is included on this amplifier PCB.

`Vpeak,rail = 12 × (mains/230) × regulation_factor × √2 − Vf`.
`ΔVpp ≈ Irail/(100×C)`; `Vaverage≈Vpeak−ΔVpp/2`.

| Scenario / assumed values | Rail peak | Approx. average | Ripple valley |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Low mains 216 V; regulation factor 1.00 at heavy load; diode 1.0 V; 0.9 A/rail; C=3,760 µF (−20%) | 14.94 V | 13.74 V | 12.55 V |
| Nominal mains 230 V; factor 1.07 at partial load; diode 0.85 V; 0.75 A/rail; C=4,700 µF | 17.31 V | 16.51 V | 15.71 V |
| High mains 253 V; no-load factor 1.10; diode 0.55 V; negligible ripple | 19.98 V | ≈19.98 V | ≈19.98 V |
| Same high/no-load case plus illustrative +3% secondary tolerance | 20.60 V | ≈20.60 V | ≈20.60 V |

An additional 0.2 V wiring drop would lower the worst valley to about 12.35 V. These are conservative selected scenarios, not a guaranteed minimum/maximum: no fixed transformer or capacitor has been selected. The 216–253 V range is an engineering assumption for this calculation, not a statement of current supply regulations. Actual regulation/tolerance, diode drop and audio-dependent current must be measured. The assumed **±21 V absolute operating ceiling** is conditional on these assumptions and on successful bias/thermal qualification. Stop and recalculate if measured rails exceed it. Use 35 V or greater local/reservoir electrolytics; 16 V parts are unsuitable.

100 VA / 24 V ≈ 4.17 Arms secondary rating. Capacitor-input rectification produces peaky RMS currents, so this is not a 4.17 A DC rating. At roughly 5 W/channel and 16 V rails, ideal class-B signal rail current per channel is Ipk/π≈0.37 A; adding about 0.06 A idle gives roughly 0.86 A per rail for stereo. Thus the 0.75–0.9 A ripple scenarios are plausible, but transformer heating and actual stereo rail sag remain measurements. The nominal 16.5 V average does not mean the amplifier always sees a stiff 16.5 V supply.

## Power, dissipation and SOA

See `SIMULATION.md` for the complete model-derived power table. At 8 Ω, P=Vpk²/16. Approximate stiff-rail useful output is 2 W at ±12 V, 4.5 W at ±15 V and 5.6 W at ±16 V. A real 12-0-12 supply should initially be treated as a roughly **5 W/channel project**, subject to measured valley rails and the release gates. Higher high-mains/no-load rails are a component stress case, not a promise of continuous stereo 10 W output. Efficiency is modest because MOSFET gate headroom limits swing: model estimates near clipping are 35%, 42% and 44% at ±12/15/16 V including front-end/idle consumption. Quiescent model draw per channel is about 1.22/1.87/2.11/3.27 W at ±12/15/16/20.5 V with one fixed adjustment; real bias must be checked at each rail.

For ideal class-B operation into a resistance, average loss in **each** output device is

`Pd = Vrail×Vpk/(πRL) − Vpk²/(4RL)`.

It peaks at Vpk=2Vrail/π, with `Pd,max=Vrail²/(π²RL)`. At 8 Ω this is 1.82/2.85/3.24/5.59 W per device at ±12/15/16/21 V. This is a continuous-sine average, not an instantaneous SOA proof. Allow an additional conservative Vrail×70 mA per device for bias/overlap: about 4.36 W/device at 16 V and 7.06 W/device at 21 V. The bound is conservative because it adds full idle loss throughout the sine wave. It does not cover runaway or shorts.

For a shared stereo sink at 40 °C ambient, four devices at 7.1 W give about 28.4 W. A sink **≤1.5 K/W**, with assumed interface resistance 1 K/W per device and RθJC≤1.15 K/W, gives sink about 83 °C and junction about 98 °C. At nominal 16 V, stereo output heat bound is about 17.4 W. These are analytical upper estimates for the specified bias and resistive load; add neighbouring heat, mounting uncertainty and enclosure restrictions. The 98 °C estimate is an average-temperature estimate. A deliberately conservative whole-die peak bound uses the approximately 13.2 W instantaneous resistive loss plus 1.5 W bias allowance continuously through RθJC: with case near 90 °C, junction stays below roughly 107 °C in that lumped model. The published transient thermal-impedance curves describe single-pulse whole-die heating; no hotspot or transient linear-SOA guarantee is inferred from them. Owned sink RθSA and mica/contact resistance are unverified. The prototype bench stop threshold of 60 °C sink temperature is deliberately lower than this analytical ceiling. No automated thermal cutoff is fitted.

The worst instantaneous resistive-load transistor loss is approximately Vrail²/[4(RL+0.33)]. At 21 V/8 Ω: about **13.2 W**, I≈1.26 A and VDS≈10.5 V on the conducting device. At the output extreme, the off device can see about 2×21=42 V. Source resistance adds modest current feedback; each 5 W resistor dissipates only roughly Ipk²×0.33/4≈0.25 W average at a 1.75 A peak sine, but its lead/copper and tolerance still matter. Reactive loads can instead combine substantial current with VDS near a full rail or more; for example 1.5 A at 21 V is 31.5 W instantaneously. A simple 8 Ω thermal average does not cover that condition.

**Linear-mode SOA cannot be signed off from these datasheets.** Both Figure 8 curves show single-pulse 100 µs/1 ms/10 ms limits at case 25 °C; neither provides a guaranteed DC curve or hot-SOA data. At 20 Hz a half-cycle is 25 ms and the amplifier is repetitively conducting, so extrapolating the 10 ms curve as DC is invalid. Whole-die thermal derating alone would scale power by (175−Tcase)/(175−25): 0.667 at case 75 °C and 0.50 at 100 °C. That does **not** guarantee a corresponding local-hotspot current limit in a switching MOSFET. The generous-looking 13 W versus 130 W headline comparison is not a valid SOA margin calculation. A quantified guaranteed hot DC SOA margin is therefore **unknown**, not passed. Obtain manufacturer linear-mode guidance or characterize conservative actual load/temperature limits before treating this as a reliable released design. Nexperia AN50006 explains why thermal instability/hotspots can invalidate an average thermal model.

4 Ω would double the ideal class-B loss bounds: 6.48 W/device at 16 V and 11.17 W/device at 21 V before bias allowance, with higher current and greater reactive SOA exposure. Although small-signal 4 Ω simulations run, **4 Ω operation is not supported by this revision**. There is no output current limiter and no short-circuit rating. Bench supply limiting does not prevent reservoir-capacitor fault current. External speaker DC protection addresses speaker DC exposure; it does not establish output-device SOA.

Other maximum nominal stresses at the assumed ceiling: small BJTs see less than the 42 V bus versus 65 V VCEO; BD139/140 less than 42 V versus 80 V. Q106 can dissipate about 0.2 W; Q107 about 0.2 W at large negative swing. A BC556 rated roughly 500 mW at 25 °C requires ambient derating; at a local 80 °C, roughly 280 mW remains using 4 mW/K derating. Keep Q107 away from the output heatsink hot spot and verify its case temperature. R108 worst approximate bus drop 40.7 V gives 0.138 W, comfortably below 0.6 W. Zobel 10 Ω loss for 14 Vpk/20 kHz is about 0.15 W; the 2 W part gives margin for normal audio, not sustained ultrasonic oscillation. The 100 nF spreader bypass sees roughly 9 V; the VAS compensation capacitor can see a much greater fraction of the rail bus, so its stocked 50 V rating is retained. No capacitors are silently replaced by lower-voltage parts.
