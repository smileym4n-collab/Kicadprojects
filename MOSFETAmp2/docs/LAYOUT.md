# Layout guidance — two-layer prototype

The original empty PCB is preserved. There is no placement, routing or layout verification. Import the schematic only after resolving the physical-fit and electrical release gates. Two layers are adequate at these currents; a four-layer board is not required to compensate for poor placement.

1. Put Q111/Q112 and Q211/Q212 along a board edge, with accessible screws and a mechanical drawing of the actual heatsink. TO-220 pins are **1 G, 2 D, 3 S**; drain tabs are live. Insulate **each** N/P device from a common sink using mica, compound and shoulder washers. A screw can bridge insulation. Verify drain-to-sink isolation before applying power. Keep the sink mechanically supported, not hanging from transistor leads.
2. Mount Q108/Q208 (BD139 bias sensors, **1 E, 2 C, 3 B**, collector tab live) on the output heatsink close to the respective pair, ideally between them. Electrically insulate them. Long remote sensor wiring creates both thermal lag and extra inductance: use a short secured three-wire connection and keep C103/C203 on the PCB beside the bias network. Each sensor tracks its own pair, not a remote point on a large stereo sink. Do not qualify bias with sensors dangling in free air.
3. Place drivers Q109/Q110 next to their gates. R117/R118 are the final series components immediately at the MOSFET gate pins; no long trace or gate branch after them. Put gate-source Zeners and 47 kΩ discharge resistors directly across the corresponding G/S pins, using short source references. Do not connect their return through the speaker-current trace. Do not add large gate test pads.
4. Put each 0R33 resistor close to its MOSFET source. Join the two resistor output ends at a compact OUT junction. Run separate quiet Kelvin traces from the source and OUT ends to the three bias test loops. TP101–TP102 and TP102–TP103 are differential measurements. Carry speaker current through wide copper, not these sense traces.
5. Take R103/R203 feedback **from OUT before L101/L201**, directly at the resistor junction. Keep this trace beside a quiet ground reference and away from MOSFET gates, speaker connector and output-coil field. The 10 Ω parallel dampers remain across the air coils. Put the series 10 Ω + 100 nF Zobel at OUT, with its return to the local power-return area. The speaker connector is after the coil and goes to the external protection PCB.
6. Put each rail's 100 nF film bypass close to the driver/output supply path. Put the two 220 µF capacitors close to the local power input/output stage; +rail capacitor positive to +V, −rail capacitor **positive to GND**. Return their charging/discharge currents locally. Main PSU reservoirs belong on the external PSU PCB.
7. Use a largely continuous GND plane. Control currents by placement: the local power connector, reservoir returns, Zobel and speaker return form the power-return region. Bring input ground and R104/R204 ground into a quiet region near the input pair; join it to the board ground without a shared narrow speaker-return neck. Both channels use the same GND net. Run substantial separate channel returns back to the external PSU star; avoid a second remote signal-ground bond that creates a large loop. Keep input shield/chassis strategy explicit in the enclosure design.
8. Place Q101/Q102 close together, matched, with flat faces bonded thermally. Keep them away from VAS heat, source resistors and airflow gradients. Put the mirror and emitter resistors nearby. Place Q115/Q106 and C102 compactly; C102 goes directly between the input-pair collector/VAS drive node and BN. Avoid routing underneath an output coil. Keep C2N and VB copper small; do not add test loops to these high-impedance nodes.
9. Rev 0.2 bias network: place D109/D112 (and D209/D212) on the PCB **next to the driver transistors** Q109/Q110, so they share the drivers' board temperature; their purpose is tracking the driver VBEs. Keep D113, Q116, Q117, R114 and RV101 together on the PCB, away from the heatsink, power resistors and coil, and away from airflow or heat gradients. The LED reference and Q116 VBE should be at similar temperature, so put D113 next to Q116. D113 is a 2012 metric SMD LED; mark its cathode on the silkscreen and check it against the actual part. Give R113 and R114 roomy through-hole pads, or sockets, because they are select-on-test parts that may be changed during commissioning. Keep the BB node (Q108 base, R112, R113, Q116 collector) compact; it is a moderate-impedance node near the VAS output.
10. Keep bias trimmers accessible to an insulated tool with the heatsink fitted. Clearly mark **MAX RESISTANCE = MIN BIAS** and **R114: FIT AFTER FIRST POWER-UP**, and mark the two-pin bias-short headers **FIT FOR FIRST POWER-UP**. The physical adjustment direction must be measured, not inferred from the schematic. Keep these headers away from speaker/output connectors to prevent accidental shunting of the wrong net.
11. Use at least 2 mm wide outer copper for short output/rail runs on 1 oz copper as an initial geometric allowance; calculate temperature rise for the final lengths and enclosure. Give live metal tabs and screw heads several millimetres mechanical clearance from adjacent copper. This is a low-voltage board, but accidental mounting shorts still destroy devices. Label power connector pin 1 +V, 2 GND, 3 −V and input/output pin 1 signal, 2 GND on both silkscreen and assembly drawing.

Air coils: start with 12 close-wound turns of 1 mm enamelled wire on an 8 mm mandrel. Wheeler's single-layer estimate, using approximately 9 mm mean diameter and 12 mm winding length, gives about **0.7 µH**. Approximate copper length 0.34 m plus leads gives roughly 8–10 mΩ DCR. Remove enamel fully at the solder joints. The provided 15.24 mm-pitch footprint is a provisional mechanical cradle, not a measured stocked component. Measure L/DCR and adjust the footprint to the actual formed coil before fabrication. Use secure supports so the wire cannot move against a live heatsink.

Before PCB order: actual package printout/fit check (pots, power resistors, film caps, terminal blocks, heatsink screws), fresh netlist, manual current-path review, native DRC after placement/routing, saved copper refill and independent Gerber/drill inspection. A matching footprint pad number does not prove the body will fit. Stability and thermal results remain measurement-dependent even after a clean DRC.
