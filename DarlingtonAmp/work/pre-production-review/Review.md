# DarlingtonAmp pre-production review — 6 October 2026

**NOT READY TO ORDER.** The basic amplifier topology is credible, but two actual PCB clearance errors and unresolved thermal/mechanical implementation prevent release. There are also component-rating, grounding, serviceability and design-intent issues worth correcting before fabrication. This is a file/datasheet/geometry review, not simulation, bench qualification or production approval.

Reviewed the saved root schematic, PCB, project settings and embedded footprints, rather than the older schematic-generation assumptions. No schematic, PCB, project or library edits were made. GUI DRC refilled zones in memory; those changes were discarded. Source hashes were checked afterwards. Review files are isolated in `work/pre-production-review`.

Coordinates below are KiCad absolute X,Y in mm, viewed from the front. The board is **81.153 × 77.258 mm**, two copper layers, nominal 1.6 mm thick. It contains 48 footprints, 110 numbered pads, 121 straight track segments, 74 track arcs, 13 zones and no vias. All tracks/arcs are 1.0 mm wide. Track lengths below include arcs but exclude pads and pours.

## BLOCKER

**B1 — Two genuine 0.20 mm clearance violations in the input/mirror area.** GUI DRC after zone refill reports:

- `Net-(Q1-E)` B.Cu arc versus **Q3 pad 3**, `/MIRROR_REF`, at **(223.266,97.790)**: **0.1547 mm** clearance. The implicated 1.5708 mm-long arc runs from (223.493817,99.240) through (222.728450,99.087759) to (222.079604,98.654214); UUID `40b86a12-2266-4ff8-8b39-819fb5090c59`.
- **Q2 pad 1**, `Net-(Q2-E)`, at **(235.712,104.394)** versus the `Net-(Q4-E)` B.Cu arc: **0.1570 mm** clearance. The 3.1416 mm-long arc runs from (235.162,106.807) through (236.576214,106.221214) to (237.162,104.807); UUID `2716be39-18f3-4180-89f1-0ad9127528ce`.

These are distinct nets, not intentional joins. **Correction:** locally move these two arcs away from the indicated pads to achieve at least 0.20 mm, preferably 0.25 mm. Alternatively narrow only the affected low-current routes to 0.6–0.8 mm with proper transitions. Refill and rerun DRC. Do not lower the clearance rule or exclude the errors. They are not evidence of a present short, but the saved board fails its intended manufacturing rule.

**B2 — Q11/Q12/Q9 heatsink arrangement is not mechanically established.** Q11/Q12 use horizontal tab-down footprints with their entire bodies over solid PCB, not overhanging an edge. Their mounting-hole centres are **Q11 (250.444,56.388)** and **Q12 (198.628,56.575)**. Q9's hole is **(224.536,77.738)**, roughly 21 mm farther down the board. The board edge is at Y=36.788; the output bodies start around Y=51.4, so there is approximately 14.6 mm of PCB between their tops and that edge. No cutouts, heat-transfer blocks or assembly drawing establish how all three devices contact the intended external sink.

A heatsink below the PCB cannot cool a tab through ordinary FR-4. A common sink must also accommodate the different TO-3P/TO-126 tab heights without straining leads, and Q9 must sense output-device temperature rather than just board air. **Correction:** establish a dimensioned assembly with the actual sink, insulators, shoulder washers, standoffs and lead bends. Either demonstrate a workable raised heat-transfer arrangement for these positions, or revise just these placements/mounting provisions to provide direct sink contact. Verify the exact Sanken MT-100 package against a 1:1 print. Do not fabricate on an assumption that the horizontal footprints alone solve mounting. If an existing mechanical arrangement already satisfies this, a verified drawing/physical fit can close this blocker without an electrical redesign.

Q11/Q12 collectors and Q9 collector must remain electrically isolated from a shared grounded sink. They are three different electrical nodes. No general PCB mounting holes exist beyond these three transistor mounting holes; define independent support if connector insertion forces would otherwise be carried by transistor leads.

## IMPORTANT

**I1 — Capacitor voltage/dielectric specification is incomplete, particularly C4.** C4 is now simply `100pF` in a **2012 metric** SMD footprint; C6/C8 are simply `220uF`. Their saved fields do not establish ratings. C4 is between `/VAS_IN` (about +33.5 to +33.9 V) and `/DRIVE_N`, which swings close to the negative rail on a negative output excursion. It can therefore experience approximately **64–67 V**, more with high/unloaded rails. A 50 V part is unsuitable here, even though its normal idle voltage is only about 32 V. The existing stock-review document records a 50 V 100 pF part; that is not an approved choice for C4.

**Correction:** explicitly specify **C4 100 pF C0G/NP0, 100 V minimum** in the present footprint; verify the exact MPN. Specify **C6/C8 220 µF, 63 V**, with dimensions compatible with D10/P5 footprints or adjust those footprints before ordering. Use suitably rated film parts for C5/C7/C9 (100 V is a conservative choice) and C10 (50 V or greater; normal voltage across it is only about 1.2 V). Verify actual maximum PSU rails before accepting any lower rating. Do not infer ratings from capacitance or footprint size.

**I2 — Quiet ground joins the power return near C8, not directly at J7.** J2.1, R2.2 and C2.2 share the bottom B.Cu ground region. Its only board return to the top ground bar runs up the left edge around X=183.6–186.2 and joins near **X=192.8, Y=39–42**, beside C8.1 at (194.310,44.704). Thus the quiet reference shares part of C8's local power-decoupling return on the way to **J7 (231.902/236.982,41.402)**. This is not a continuous reference plane, and is not a true independent quiet return to the connector star. It can convey negative-rail decoupling voltage into the input reference. No numerical hum/distortion claim can be made from geometry alone.

**Correction:** retain one GND net, but route the J2.1/R2.2/C2.2 quiet-ground region by a dedicated branch directly to a defined J7 star point. Keep the C8/C9, C6/C7 and R21 power returns on their own approaches to that point. Remove the old quiet-branch join at the C8 end if adding the direct branch, so this does not become an uncontrolled loop. Keep the external loudspeaker return on a separate heavy wire to the PSU reservoir star, together with a separate board-J7 return. Do not send loudspeaker current through J2 or the left/bottom quiet-ground path. Label this external wiring requirement in the assembly documentation.

**I3 — The saved input is AC-coupled, contrary to the requested DC-coupled input description.** J2.2 goes through **C1=2.2 µF**, then R3=100 Ω to Q1 base, with R2=22 kΩ to ground. C1 is populated, and no input bypass/link remains. The old note “DC input link fitted” is false. The C1 Engineering field says to remove R1 to fit C1, but **R1 is now the dual output emitter resistor**. Following that stale instruction would disable the output stage.

**Correction:** remove the stale R1/input-link instructions. Confirm the intended input coupling: retaining C1 is electrically reasonable but must be documented as AC input coupling; to implement the stated DC input path, explicitly designate a link across C1's two pads (or a separately defined bypass) and update the schematic/BOM accordingly. Do not remove the present R1. The power amplifier has direct coupling between its active stages and at its output either way.

**I4 — C4/VAS and NFB routes are longer than desirable for a first analogue-power prototype.** Q8 base is at (233.045,80.391), while C4.1 is at (226.590,93.345); the `/VAS_IN` network totals **28.8 mm** and the `/DRIVE_N` network **49.3 mm**. R10.1 is at (216.408,94.742), Q2 base at (230.632,104.394), and R11.1 at (197.612,88.900); `/NFB` totals **41.4 mm**. R11 and C2 are far left of Q2, giving the sensitive feedback network a large footprint. This is a parasitic-coupling/stability concern, not proof of oscillation.

**Correction:** shorten the local C4 connections to Q8 base/collector and bring R10/R11 toward Q2 base, with C2's reference returned to the quiet node. Preserve the existing direct `/AMP_OUT` sense from R1.2 and avoid bundling NFB with DRIVE_N/DRIVE_P or the rail feeder tracks. This can be a localized front-end placement/routing improvement, not a topology change. The short output base-stopper-to-base runs should be retained.

**I5 — Output bypass loops are physically remote from collectors.** C6/C7 and C8/C9 are at Y≈43–48 while Q11/Q12 collectors are at Y≈73, roughly 25–30 mm away. The broad rail strips help resistance, but loop inductance remains. The VAS/front-end supplies are fed farther down these rails; C10 only bypasses the reference and is not a substitute for rail bypassing.

**Correction:** move the existing HF rail bypass capacitors C7/C9 closer to the Q11/Q12 collector feed regions and minimize their rail/return loop; retain bulk C6/C8 near the power entry with low-impedance returns. Evaluate additional local small-signal rail bypass provision near R12/R13 only if needed after this layout change; no automatic filter/topology change is required. Keep these returns out of the quiet-reference branch described in I2.

**I6 — Salvage/mechanical and power-part qualifications remain incomplete.** R1's measured footprint has 10 mm adjacent pitch, 31×5 mm body and 1.3 mm holes; L1 has 12 mm pitch, 15×15 mm body and 2.3 mm holes. The L1 footprint README explicitly says its pin-to-body-edge offset was **assumed 1.5 mm**, and R1's watts per section are unknown. At 60 W/8 Ω, each R1 half dissipates about **0.825 W average**; a 30 Vpk/4 Ω sine produces about **3.09 W per half**. L1 must carry about 2.74 Arms at 60 W/8 Ω and over 5 Arms in the hypothetical 4 Ω case. Inductance alone does not establish current or heat capability.

**Correction:** dry-fit these owned parts on a 1:1 print, verify the stated R1 common terminal and both resistances, confirm L1 body offset/lead diameter/height, measure its DCR, and establish R1 power/temperature capability. Use at least a conservatively rated 3 W per R1 section for an 8 Ω build, preferably 5 W if supported by the actual part; do not claim 4 Ω capability merely from body size. R21/R22 must be suitable non-inductive power types (at least 2 W initial specification) whose actual body/lead pitch fit the 11.9 mm/P15.24 footprints. If suitable parts do not fit, correct the footprints before order.

**I7 — Connector mechanics need final confirmation.** J4–J7 have 5.08 mm leg spacing and 1.43 mm holes. The [TE 62409-1 page](https://www.te.com/en/product-62409-1.html) lists 1.4 mm holes, a 6.35×0.81 mm mating tab, and **3.18 mm recommended board thickness**, whereas this PCB is 1.6 mm. This is a retention/assembly qualification issue, not proof the terminal cannot be soldered. The exact customer drawing and fitted connector must settle lead engagement and underside protrusion. Their reusable `Spade connector` library is not in the project-local fp-lib-table, although embedded footprints allow manufacture.

**Correction:** verify the actual terminal on a 1.6 mm board coupon/assembly drawing, provide mechanical support and underside clearance, and preserve its footprint locally for reproducibility. J2 is JST-XH **2.50 mm**, not a generic 2.54 mm header. Use the correct mating housing; do not substitute the previously noted Multicomp connector by pitch approximation.

**I8 — 4 Ω and fault operation are unqualified; external protection is part of the build.** There is no onboard current limiter, DC speaker disconnect, thermal shutdown or rail fuse. A single pair at ±35 V is credible for the intended 8 Ω amplifier with adequate cooling; it is **not approved here for sustained full-power 4 Ω or arbitrary reactive loads**. Shorted output current is not constrained to a safe device SOA by R1 or the 33 Ω base resistors.

**Correction:** specify this revision for 8 Ω, keep 4 Ω testing brief/reduced-voltage until hot SOA and thermal behaviour are validated, and define external rail fusing, DC speaker protection/startup delay and thermal operating limits before normal loudspeaker use. Protection can remain external; this finding does not require a broad PCB redesign. Bench supply current limiting does not automatically protect transistors from energy in charged local/external capacitors.

## TUNE DURING TESTING

**T1 — DC operating point and bias.** The following are hand estimates at ±35 V, shorted input, balanced devices and approximately 0.6–0.7 V small-signal VBE. They are expected diagnostic ranges, not simulated results or guaranteed limits.

Q1/Q2 each dissipate roughly 30–37 mW, Q5 about 60–70 mW, and Q6/Q7 below 1 mW each. Q3/Q4 have low collector-emitter voltage and only a few mW dissipation. R9 sees approximately 69 V and dissipates about **70 mW**; a properly voltage-rated 0.25 W or larger part is adequate electrically. R8 is about 1 mW; R12/R13 about 3–5 mW. No small-signal transistor is near its nominal current or voltage limit in the estimated normal idle state. Q8/Q10 remain warm enough to avoid deliberately heating Q1/Q2 with them.

At DC C2 opens the lower feedback leg, so the amplifier core's noise/signal gain from Q1 base is approximately unity. R2 and R10 both being 22k provides useful input-bias-current cancellation for matched Q1/Q2. With collector currents around 0.9 mA, base currents can be roughly 1.3–4.5 µA across gain groups; unmatched devices can therefore leave tens of millivolts, potentially around 70 mV of bias-current mismatch contribution, plus VBE mismatch, leakage and thermal error. Do not promise a few millivolts from nominal values. Match Q1/Q2 at the actual operating current and keep their temperatures equal. Their body centres are approximately 6.6 mm apart; a shared thermal sleeve/clip is preferable to relying solely on proximity.

**T2 — Darlington bias range and failure behaviour.** Q9 is a conventional NPN VBE multiplier: collector=DRIVE_N, emitter=DRIVE_P, R14=2k2 collector-to-base, and R15=2k2 in parallel with R16+RV1 from base to emitter. RV1 pins 2 and 3 are both connected to DRIVE_P, including on PCB.

`Vspread ≈ VBE9 × [2 + 2200/(680+RV1)]` ignoring Q9 base current. At VBE9=0.65 V, RV1=5k gives **1.55 V**, RV1=0 gives **3.40 V**. Q9 base current raises these figures; for an illustrative 6 mA multiplier current and beta 25, they are about **2.02 V and 3.80 V**. The real selected device should be measured. The range is plausible for two integrated Darlingtons, whose total bias is several junction drops, not the ~1.3 V assumed for two ordinary BJTs. The upper adjustment can produce excessive current and is not a safe operating endpoint.

An open wiper retains the full track through pin 3 and tends toward minimum bias. An open RV1/shunt branch leaves R15 and tends lower still. This does not protect against a shorted track or open R15/Q9 failure. Start at **maximum RV1 resistance between pins 1 and 3**, not an assumed clockwise direction. Q9's approximate temperature slope, multiplied by a factor around 3–4 at the useful setting, is plausibly comparable with the four output junctions, but thermal lag and the integrated 70 Ω resistor make hot testing essential. A cold, remote Q9 is not adequate compensation.

At 80–100 mA, **each 0.22 Ω section must read 17.6–22.0 mV**. For the actual R1 mapping: measure positive voltage from **R1.3 to R1.2** for Q11, and **R1.2 to R1.1** for Q12. Across R1.3 to R1.1 expect **35.2–44.0 mV**. Use these voltages, not total rail current, to set output bias.

**T3 — Stability and compensation.** C4=100 pF Miller compensation is a plausible conservative starting point; retain C0G and adequate voltage rating. Differential-pair degeneration gives an effective differential transconductance on the order of 7–8 mS; a crude dominant-pole estimate gives unity-loop crossover around **0.6 MHz** at noise gain 20.13. That estimate ignores transistor poles, load capacitance and wiring; it cannot establish phase margin. The input-stage current divided by C4 gives a large-signal slew-rate scale of order 10–20 V/µs, above the approximately 3.9 V/µs required for 31 Vpk at 20 kHz, but this also needs measurement.

C3=10 pF lead compensation is DNP **and explicitly excluded from the PCB**, so there is no C3 tuning footprint. If fitted later, its R10·C3 corner is approximately 723 kHz and it changes high-frequency feedback substantially. Do not fit it speculatively. Restore a nearby DNP footprint before manufacture if that tuning option is desired.

R17/R18=33 Ω are sensible initial base stoppers and are about **5.23/5.06 mm** of trace from Q11/Q12 bases. Keep those short runs. C5=100 nF in series with R21=10 Ω forms the Zobel before L1; its RC corner is approximately **159 kHz**. L1=0.95 µH is shunted by R22=10 Ω; its reactance is **0.119 Ω at 20 kHz**, and equals 10 Ω at **1.68 MHz**. These are credible output-isolation/damping values. L1's physical construction/DCR and resistor inductance remain qualification items.

Initial tests: use an 8 Ω non-inductive dummy load; inspect 1 kHz sine and low-amplitude 1 kHz/10 kHz square waves, then a 20 kHz sine at progressively higher level. Examine both AMP_OUT and SPK_OUT using short probe grounds. Check no-load operation, then introduce 10 nF and 100 nF in parallel with 8 Ω at SPK_OUT at low amplitude and reduced rails, watching for sustained ringing/oscillation. Stop immediately for sustained HF activity or abnormal supply current. Do not begin with a large pure capacitive load or full-power square waves. Check clipping recovery and startup/shutdown/unequal-rail behaviour separately. All C4/C3 tuning, bias drift and capacitive-load claims remain open until measured.

**T4 — Output power, SOA and cooling.** Taking **30–31 Vpk** as a reasonable target swing after device/emitter-resistor headroom gives **56.3–60.1 W into 8 Ω**, 3.75–3.875 Apk and 2.65–2.74 Arms. **65 W requires 32.25 Vpk** and should not be promised with ±35 V rails, especially after PSU sag. Required input is approximately 1.05–1.09 Vrms for this output; a 2 Vrms source can overdrive it.

An idealized 30 Vpk/4 Ω case would demand **7.5 Apk, 5.30 Arms and 112.5 W**; this is a stress calculation, not a rated output claim. Additional device and emitter-resistor losses reduce attainable swing.

For resistive Class-B operation, per-device average dissipation is approximately `35·Vpk/(π·RL) − Vpk²/(4·RL)`. Its maximum is **15.5 W/device at 8 Ω** and **31.0 W/device at 4 Ω**, at Vpk≈22.3 V. Allow margin for Class-AB overlap, PSU tolerance, device imbalance and reactive current. Peak instantaneous resistive dissipation is roughly **38.3 W at 8 Ω / 76.6 W at 4 Ω**, near Vout=17.5 V. Idle is not the hottest sustained sine-wave condition.

The [Sanken output datasheets](https://www.semicon.sanken-ele.co.jp/sk_content/2sb1560_ds_en.pdf) show 150 V VCEO, 10 A IC and 100 W only at a 25°C case, with DC and single-pulse SOA curves. Both local manufacturer graphs were visually inspected. Around 17.5 V VCE, the resistive 8 Ω load-line current (~2.19 A) lies comfortably below the cold DC boundary; the 4 Ω point (~4.38 A) has much less hot margin. The DC boundary is of order 3 A at 35 V on the printed graph, not 10 A. Treat these as graph-read approximations.

A simple 100 W/125°C-rise thermal derating gives only about 60 W at a 75°C case; the 4 Ω peak dissipation already exceeds that continuous thermal limit. Repetitive low-frequency and reactive operation cannot be authorized using single-pulse curves. A 90° reactive-current example at zero output voltage can put ~35 V and 3.75 A simultaneously on one device even for the 8 Ω current scale, about 131 W instantaneous. Pulse duration, phase, frequency and case temperature must be assessed. There is no short-circuit SOA guarantee.

For an 8 Ω prototype, an illustrative shared sink target is **about 1°C/W or better in the actual enclosure**, assuming ~40 W total design heat, 40°C internal ambient, inferred RθJC≈1.25°C/W and ~0.5°C/W case-to-sink per output device: junctions could then be around 115°C at 20 W/device. This is a sizing illustration, not a measured heatsink specification; verify the actual interface and sink. Full-power 4 Ω would require materially stronger cooling and hot SOA evidence even then.

**T5 — Practical first power-up, only after correcting/closing the pre-order items.**

1. Mount Q11/Q12/Q9 in the verified thermal arrangement. Check isolation to sink, supply polarity and absence of shorts. Attach meter clips with power off. Short J2 input signal to J2 ground; no loudspeaker or dummy load initially. Set RV1 to maximum end-to-wiper resistance, verified with a meter; circuit parallel paths may affect an in-circuit reading.
2. Start with tracking **±10–12 V**, roughly **50 mA current limit per rail**. Minimum-bias current should be well below the final target, typically front-end/VAS current plus small output conduction. Stop if a rail remains limited, output sits near a rail, or oscillation appears; do not simply increase the limit.
3. Verify AMP_OUT near zero, both emitter-resistor voltages low, sensible VAS/tail node voltages relative to the reduced rails, and no HF oscillation. Raise rails together in stages to ±35 V while still at minimum bias. Check startup and shutdown behaviour with no speaker.
4. If stable, allow about **150 mA per rail**, then increase bias slowly toward **17.6 mV across each half of R1 (80 mA)**. Approach 22 mV/100 mA only if thermal behaviour warrants it. Do not set bias solely from the PSU display.
5. Warm for at least 20–30 minutes on the real sink; repeatedly check bias, DC offset and temperature. Stop for continuing upward bias drift. Use an initial acceptance target of **under ±50 mV steady DC**, preferably under ±20 mV after matching; this is a test criterion, not a prediction.
6. Power down before fitting the 8 Ω dummy load; repeat low-level tests, then increase signal/current limit only as required. Scope clipping, ringing, capacitive-load response and thermal drift. Before connecting a loudspeaker, verify external DC protection/startup delay and satisfactory start/stop transients. Connect the speaker return directly to the PSU star.

## PASS

**P1 — Signal topology and feedback polarity.** Q1/Q2 are NPN 2SC2240 differential inputs with R6/R7=100 Ω degeneration and Q5 tail sink. Q3/Q4 are PNP 2SA970 mirror loads, with Q4 diode-connected and Q3 supplying the VAS input node. Q8 is a PNP common-emitter VAS; Q10 is its NPN current sink. Q6/Q7 form the two-junction reference. These are the parts actually present, not KSC1845/KSA992. There is no separate output-driver pair: Q11/Q12 are the integrated Darlingtons.

A positive Q1 input increases its collector current, pulls VAS_IN downward, increases PNP Q8 conduction and drives the output upward. A rising output raises Q2 base through R10, steers current toward Q2 and drives the corrective output change downward. Therefore the global loop is negative feedback. R10 senses **R1.2/AMP_OUT before L1**, as intended by the schematic note; it is not accidentally taken after the inductor. The approximately **20.9 mm** sense trace leaves the emitter-resistor common pad separately from the power pour toward L1. This is a useful Kelvin-style takeoff.

**P2 — Gain and frequency-setting values.** Midband gain is `1+22000/1150 = 20.1304 V/V = 26.077 dB`. C2=220 µF gives a feedback shelving pole near **0.629 Hz**; the core approaches unity DC gain. C1=2.2 µF with R2=22k gives an input high-pass pole near **3.29 Hz** for a low-impedance source, and input impedance is approximately 22k in band. The saved connector-to-output DC transfer is zero because of C1. At 20 Hz the two LF networks together give approximately 0.12 dB attenuation relative to midband. The output remains directly connected to the load through R1/L1; no output coupling electrolytic exists.

**P3 — Device polarity and electrical pin mapping.** All 48 intended onboard references and 110 numbered PCB pads were compared against the fresh netlist; no extra/missing onboard reference or pad/net mismatch was found. C3 is intentionally `on_board no`, so its absence is accounted for. This independent comparison complements GUI connectivity and does not replace physical package verification.
